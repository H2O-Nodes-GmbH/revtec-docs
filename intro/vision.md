# Vision

## Short Term: Volatile, Increasing REV

In the short term, REV will remain volatile, mirroring changes in market activity and creating opportunities for revSOL traders and yield farmers. Over time, Solana should solidify its position as a leading chain for high-volume financial activity and continue to produce more REV.

## Medium Term: Major Catalysts

Two events will serve as major catalysts for RevTec users and for staking on Solana more broadly:

* **SIMD-123 implementation:** Once SIMD-123 is live on mainnet, sharing block rewards from validators to stakers becomes a normal, in-protocol path. That should raise awareness of how large those rewards can be. Validators will need to reassess fee splits to stay competitive — generally lowering fees and raising staking yields for stakers.
* **Issuance reduction:** Eventually, the Solana community is likely to reduce issuance (a successor to SIMD-228). REV would then become a larger share of default staking yield. That mix would also become more volatile, making stakers more aware of REV’s dynamics and more aligned with Solana’s on-chain activity.

Together, those events should put REV in the spotlight. RevTec will offer clear, differentiated liquid staking tokens based on staker preferences, with competitive fees.

## Long Term: New Investor Classes

RevTec is a first step toward differentiated products built on staking yield. Staking yield is the largest yield source in crypto, but many yield investors find it hard to use: it is volatile, often opaque, and poorly differentiated. RevTec’s two liquid staking tokens are building blocks for pure-yield and [fixed income](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1eiU8qxOxyZZ0D295bFGt8RVw2M8R0jOz/view?usp=sharing) products. Combined with hedging, dollar-denominated yield products could scale large enough to attract a new class of investors to Solana and deepen participation in its capital markets.
